# Date Due ## Thesis A task is a record with a history, not a row that blinks out of existence. This concept refuses the category's disappearing list — the item that fades on completion, the suggestion chip that vanishes when dismissed, the undo that lives four seconds in a toast and then is gone. Here every act leaves a dated mark on the slip: a part you accepted is stamped with the day you accepted it, a part you struck stays legible with a line through it, a completed task keeps every mark it earned. The product asks you to trust a list you did not sort. Trust comes from being able to see what happened to it. ## World The university library circulation system — the date-due slip inside the back cover, the card pocket, the accession stamp, the rubber strike that lands a degree off square. The administrative instrument, not the reading room. - `#D7C6A2` kraft is the shell's committed ground — a real card stock carrying whole regions, not a grey screen with an accent. - `#EFE6D2` card and `#E6DAC0` card-2 raise records, inputs and the nav rail off that field. - `#241C2E` stamp ink is the text and the marks (9.9:1 on kraft). `#575046` graphite is the apparatus voice (4.7:1 on kraft). - `#A8271C` vermilion is reserved for stamp strikes and section rules; `#8E1F16` is its small-text weight (5.3:1 on kraft, 7.2:1 on card). Vermilion means past due and nothing else. - `#2C4A6E` set-blue is the one cool ink and means exactly one thing: a part that has been accepted and written to the record. **Public Sans** sets the interface and the user's own sentences; **Oswald**, condensed and letterspaced in caps, sets every date, count, label and stamp — the rubber-stamp voice. Tabular numerals throughout, because a date-due slip is a column of dates and the column must align. Density: tight stamped rows on hairlines, generous separation between groups, no card inside a card, no rounded corners anywhere but the one standard toggle. ## Signature move **Every state is stamped, dated, and never removed.** Accepting a part strikes `SET 16 NOV` across it in set-blue and turns its value blue (`m4`, `m5`, `d3`, `d4`, `d5`). Rejecting strikes the value through and leaves it readable with its reason beneath. Past due is the `OVERDUE` strike in vermilion, rotated off square, sitting on the row itself (`m1`, `m7`, `d1`). A pending suggestion is an unfilled `PENDING` outline — the one mark that also appears offline (`m2`). Completing keeps every mark; the archive is the card pocket, not a wastebasket (`d6`). Three disciplines carried in from directions this one beat: **Live readout** — the apparatus states its own numbers in Oswald caps rather than hiding them: counts per list, the reference date, what has been stamped and struck, and in Settings the fact that the Fixture has not been scored yet. **Nothing disappears, it cancels** — every removal is a mark, not an absence. **The pending queue clears in place** — on the phone, reviewing advances without navigating. ## Accept / edit / reject Each part is its own bordered block carrying the part name, the engine that proposed it, the proposed value, and three equal buttons: `ACCEPT` `EDIT` `REJECT`. Same size, same weight, always visible, never behind a disclosure. `Accept all` / `Reject all` sit apart below a rule, in plain outline, under the line "Shortcuts. They do nothing the three blocks above cannot do one at a time." The dominant arrangement on the screen is per-part action, which is what the PRD's counter-metric against an acceptance-rate-optimised UI actually requires. Accepting stamps the block, turns the value blue, and puts a real `UNDO` button in the block — one step, restoring the previous value (`m5`, `d4`). Editing replaces the value with a standard segmented control and `SAVE` / `CANCEL`, and writes your pick rather than the proposal (`m4`, `d3`). Rejecting strikes the value and notes that it will not be proposed again for this task. Priority is a stack of three bars — filled by level — beside its word, so it survives without colour. Engine provenance is a boxed `R` or `M` plus the name spelled out. Past due carries the word `OVERDUE`, not just vermilion. Nothing in this concept is encoded by colour alone. ## 360 and 1280 At 360 the slip is a single column: record at the top, three part-blocks stacked, shortcuts beneath, and a standard bottom tab bar. At 1280 the shell becomes a left nav rail of derived lists and a **right apparatus column** — the live readout that has no room on the phone. The part-blocks widen rather than multiply, so the review reads identically at both widths while the desktop gains the running commentary: what is stamped, what is struck, what is still open, what has left the device. The phone drops the apparatus column, not the controls. ## Honest risk Kraft is the hardest ground in this round to keep accessible: vermilion small text fails 4.5:1 on it unless darkened, which is why this concept carries two vermilions, and any later palette drift silently breaks contrast. The stamped history is also a bet that accumulation reads as trust rather than as clutter — on a task reviewed, edited and re-suggested several times, the marks will pile up, and the concept has no answer yet for the slip that gets too busy. And a library card is a nostalgic object: it risks reading as decoration to someone who wants a fast list, which is exactly the user this product has.

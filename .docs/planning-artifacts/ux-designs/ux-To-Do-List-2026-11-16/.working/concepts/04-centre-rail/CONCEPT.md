# Centre Rail

## Thesis

A reference edition drawn by a ruling engine: short-measure columns of roman type held small
inside a wide annotation margin, twinned around a hairline channel that carries the suggestion's
three parts as square detents. **Every state is a printed mark, never a colour wash.** It refuses
the category arrangement — a full-bleed list that grows to the viewport, state told by coloured
pills, and one dominant Accept button — and it refuses that arrangement's quieter cousin, a centred
max-width column with dead grey gutters.

## World

Three values, held as the concept's discipline.

- **Bone `#F2EDE3`** — the ground on every frame. Light, because the scene is a phone between
  classes and a cold tram at eight in the morning, not a night dashboard.
- **Iron `#17171A`** — all ink: text, every rule, every control border, every drawn icon. 15.3:1.
- **Vermilion `#A6261C`** — the rubric, and only three jobs: list and section headings plus the
  active nav mark; what the engine proposed and the user has not yet confirmed; the detent pressed
  home. Past-due dates take it too, with an underline. 6.2:1.

No greys, no tints, no second accent. Placeholder text is never greyed — it is set in the
apparatus voice, so it reads as a label rather than as faint content.

**Type.** Spectral for text: task text 17–19px on 1.5, measure 34–40 characters. Archivo for the
apparatus at 9–11px, uppercase, letterspaced 0.08–0.16em, and for every numeral, tabular. No
italic. No radius on anything. Every rule exactly one device pixel, on a half-pixel coordinate.

**Density.** The text block never grows: two 320px columns at 1280, one 286px column at 360. The
margin absorbs the width — 268px of inhabited apparatus at 1280 — rather than the measure
stretching.

## Signature move

**The centre rail.** A 1px vertical channel with square detents cut into it wherever the flow
changes footing, and letter-keys printed on the rail above each detent. Hairline square at rest;
solid vermilion when pressed; bracketed on focus; struck with a bar when rejected or spent; run
through with a rule when unavailable. It carries T / P / S on `m2`–`m5` and `d2`–`d4`, the task's
accepted fields on `m6` and `d5`, the two engines as R / M on `m8`, `d7`, `d8`, and footing cuts
alone on the list frames. The full key is printed in `d2`'s margin and below `m3`'s content.

## Accept / edit / reject

Each part is a band across both columns: left is what the record holds now, right is what the
engine proposes. Three identical buttons — ACCEPT, EDIT, REJECT, 96px, same border, no fill, no
primary — sit under each proposal. Accept All / Reject All are underlined text links below the last
band, carrying the line "shortcuts — they do nothing the three rows above cannot". Accepting fills
the detent and brackets the value out of the proposal column into the record column; the right side
then reads ACCEPTED beside a live UNDO and "single step — restores the previous value". Rejecting
strikes the proposal and prints "unchanged — the rejection wrote nothing". Editing opens a standard
select, drawn open with `high` chosen, APPLY / CANCEL, and "editing writes your value, not the
proposal". `d3`/`m4` show the other two parts untouched.

Because the vocabulary is printed marks, the product's open requirement is met structurally rather
than by addition: priority is a bottom-aligned stack of 1px rules — three, two, one — plus the
word; provenance is the engine named in words plus a drawn mark (Rule Engine a ruled square, Model
Engine a square crossed off-axis); selection and the active nav item take a filled square as well
as the rubric. Colour never carries any of them alone.

## 360 and 1280

Not a reflow. At 1280 the rail runs *between* the columns and is read across: record left, proposal
right, one part per band. At 360 there is one column, the rail moves **outboard** down the left
edge, the two columns collapse into a vertical order — current value right-aligned on the label
row, proposal beneath, buttons beneath that — and the apparatus that lived in the margin moves
*below* the content: the state key, the engine line, the counts. Buttons are 90 × 32 and sit clear
of the bottom nav, so one part's accept/edit/reject is one thumb reach, with no horizontal scroll
at either width. Motion, where it exists, is the rail: one damped axis, no overshoot.

## Honest risk

Three values and no colour coding costs real glanceability on a product whose four tag contexts and
three priority levels all want telling apart at speed. Marks and position carry priority well — the
rule-stack is scannable down a column and never ambiguous — and they carry part-state and
provenance very well, because those are few and always labelled. **Tags are where it strains:**
`school` / `group` / `work` / `personal` are separated by their word alone, in 10px caps inside
brackets, so a twenty-task list is read rather than glanced. A four-mark tag alphabet was
considered and rejected as unlearnable; the honest mitigation is the per-tag Smart Lists in the
nav, which is navigation, not recognition. Second cost: hairlines and 9px letterspaced apparatus
are unforgiving. A half-pixel misalignment shows, and this density needs production discipline that
a one-person course project may not have time for.

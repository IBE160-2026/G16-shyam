# Kurs

## Thesis

Your sentence and the machine's proposal stay two separate objects on the screen, and the proposal
is laid *over* the record rather than mixed into it. That separation is the product's central promise
made visible: the course layer never edits the terrain, which is the same rule as the app never
rewriting what you typed. This concept refuses the arrangement where a suggestion arrives as an
inline chip row inside the task — because once the proposal sits inside the record, nothing on screen
says it is not yours yet.

It is built from your decisions. Kartet supplies the whole visual world. The borrowed frames keep
their skeletons: Date Due's sectioned list and settings page (`m1`, `m8`), its three-part-block
review with undo (`m5`); Tett's module structure for capture, task detail and lists (`m2`, `m6`,
`m7`) and its full mosaic, reviews and settings on desktop (`d1`, `d2`, `d4`, `d7`, `d8`); Kartet's own review and
archive (`m3`, `m4`, `d3`, `d5`, `d6`). What the imports lose is their *state vocabulary* — Date
Due's rubber stamp and Tett's red tab are gone, replaced by punching a numbered control. One
mechanic owns every frame.

## World

Kartet's, unchanged and not reinterpreted — you pinned it for colours, text and sizing, so every
value is lifted from `03-kartet/palette.json`.

Chrome is map-margin paper `#EDE4D3`; the work area is runnable ground `#FCFBF8` with fields in
white, because white means settled. Terrain ink `#1B1915`, secondary `#5C5345`. Contour brown is
rules only — `#CBB08E` hairline, `#9B6B3F` emphasised. Open yellow `#F2CB5A` is the due-this-week
window, thicket olive `#4E7033` is Need Attention and appears nowhere else, marsh `#6BA3D6` /
`#1F5878` is the inert — the archive, and an engine not in use. Course purple `#8E2E7E` with wash
`#F8EAF4` is reserved absolutely for a proposal not yet accepted.

**Barlow Condensed** 700 sets control numerals, band labels, nav and all apparatus; **Archivo**
400–600 sets task text and body. Tabular figures on every date and count. Density is Kartet's: 22px
band rows, 46px mobile records, hairlines instead of card edges, no nested boxes.

## Signature move

**Punching the controls, on every surface.** The proposal is a purple-ruled panel offset from the
record, carrying three numbered controls joined by purple legs — 1 Tags, 2 Priority, 3 Summary — with
a dashed leg from the record to control 1. Accepting *punches* the control: the circle fills with
ink, pin-pricks appear, the value settles into the record in the terrain's own ink, and the purple
leaves that part. Rejecting strikes the circle through and leaves it readable. A start triangle marks
any task with a control still open.

The imports inherit it rather than keeping their own marks. Date Due's dated stamp becomes a punched
control carrying its date (`m5`, `d5`); Tett's review table carries control numerals and punch
glyphs in its first column (`d2`, `d4`); the task detail's history reads as a control description
(`m6`, `d5`).

## Accept / edit / reject

Each control is its own block, carrying the part name, the engine that proposed it, the proposed
value, and three equal buttons: `Accept` `Edit` `Reject`. Same size, same weight, always visible,
never behind a disclosure. `Punch all` / `Strike all` sit below a rule in plain outline, under the
line "shortcuts · they do nothing the three controls cannot".

Punching turns the value to terrain ink, stamps it `punched 16 nov`, and puts a real `Undo` button in
the block — one step, restoring the previous value (`m5`, `d4`). Editing replaces the value with a
standard segmented control plus `Apply` / `Cancel`, and writes your pick rather than the proposal
(`m4`, `d3`). Striking leaves the field untouched and notes that the control is not proposed again.
A status row under the sheet shows all three control-states at a glance.

Priority is a three-bar ladder beside its word; engine provenance is a boxed `R` or `M` plus the name
spelled out; past due carries the word as well as the olive. Nothing is encoded by colour alone, and
every frame publishes the legend that says so.

## 360 and 1280

At 360 the course panel sits **below** the record, full width, controls stacked and joined by a
vertical purple leg. On the dedicated review screen at 1280 (`d3`) it sits **beside** the record,
with a dashed leg crossing the gap — the terrain column stays a short measure and the panel takes
the width it gains. The spatial relationship is the same at both widths: two objects, one laid over
the other. What changes is the axis.

Inside the desktop mosaic (`d2`, `d4`) the course becomes a purple-ruled **table module** in the
grid, one row per control, because that is the structure you picked from Tett for those two frames.
It is the same mechanic in a different container: the controls are still numbered, still punched,
still acted on one at a time, and the `In the record now` module beside it shows what each punch
actually wrote.

## Honest risk

Two objects cost vertical space, and the phone pays. The review surface is the fullest screen in the
app at 360, and a fourth part would not fit without scrolling the panel away from the record it
belongs to — which is the one thing this concept cannot afford. The panel also goes quiet on every
surface with no proposal open: on `m1`, `m7` and `d6` the purple simply is not there, so the world's
most distinctive device is absent from roughly half the app, and those frames lean on the terrain
inks alone to stay recognisable.
